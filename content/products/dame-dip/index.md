Dame Dip is Dame's entry-level internal-and-external vibrator, sitting at the lower end of the brand's price range (44 USD, against 103 USD for [Pom](/products/dame-pom/)) rather than being a specialist shape like the curved [Arc](/products/dame-arc/), the air-pulse [Aer](/products/dame-aer/), the wand [Com](/products/dame-com/) or the wearable [Eva II](/products/dame-eva-ii/). Dame's comparison widget labels Dip "Best for entry-level buyers wanting simple internal + external use," and its Shopify data lists it as "Dip Basic Vibrator," product type "Basic Vibrator."

Venus has not physically tested this product. This review is based on Dame's official Dip product page and its published specifications, checked on 12 October 2026. Where Dame does not document something, this page says so rather than filling the gap.

## Quick Verdict

Dip is the pick for someone who wants a straightforward, documented, affordably priced vibrator for both internal and external use, rather than a toy with multiple pattern modes or a specialist curve. The specification page covers the essentials: medical-grade silicone badged 100% body-safe, waterproof construction, 5 intensity levels on a single button, USB-C charging with a 1.5-hour runtime from a 2-hour charge, published dimensions of 5.8 x 1.3 in. (about 147 x 33 mm), and a 3-year warranty. The trade-off for the lower price is simplicity: only a single steady vibration pattern, against 5 pattern modes on Pom, Com and Arc. It also prices in US dollars (44.00 USD), states waterproofing without an IP rating, gives no decibel figure behind its "whisper quiet" description, and does not publish a usable product weight.

## Format and Who It Is For

Dip is a single-body vibrator for both internal and external use: Dame markets it as a "Versatile Internal & External Vibrator" with an ergonomic shape for shallow penetration and clitoral play. The body is a smooth, gently curved silicone shaft that stands upright on its own base, without a handle, flared ridges or a secondary motor head; a single raised button powers it on, steps through 5 intensity levels, and powers it off - simpler than the two-button, level-plus-pattern schemes on Pom, Com and Arc.

It suits buyers who want an affordable, no-frills entry point into Dame's range and don't need multiple patterns or app control. Buyers who want a curve aimed at G-spot stimulation should look at the [Arc](/products/dame-arc/); those who want a palm-held device with no insertable part should look at the [Pom](/products/dame-pom/); and anyone who wants a flexible-head wand should look at the [Com](/products/dame-com/).

## Documented Specifications

Dame's Specifications block lists the material as "Medical-Grade Silicone," with a separate "100% Body-safe" badge elsewhere on the page. The device offers 5 intensity levels, cycled through the single control button. Dame's Dip-vs-Pom comparison widget labels Dip's pattern count "1 Steady Pattern," against Pom's "5 Vibration Patterns" in the same row; this review uses that documented figure for the patterns field rather than leaving it unstated.

Power is USB-C rechargeable, with a run time of 1.5 hours at the highest settings from a 2-hour charge; Dame adds a reminder to use a wall adapter rated 5V or lower and keep the device dry before charging. A low-power indicator is also listed. The box contains the Dip vibrator, a USB-C cable, a storage bag and a user manual; two colors are listed, Plum and Periwinkle.

Dimensions are given as "5.8\" L x 1.3\" D," about 147 x 33 mm. Only two measurements are published - length and diameter, consistent with Dip's rounded, cylindrical body - and the same figure appears again, unchanged, in Dame's Dip-vs-Pom comparison widget, so there is no conflicting figure to flag here. Dame does not publish a product weight, an app, a noise level in decibels, or a travel lock for Dip; a Shopify variant field lists a numeric "weight," but it is recorded as 0 for both colors, not a usable figure, so it is not used here.

## Materials, Waterproofing and Cleaning

Dip's body is medical-grade silicone, badged 100% body-safe, non-porous and appropriate for internal and external intimate use when cleaned correctly. The Specifications block describes it simply as waterproof, without an IP rating. Clean with mild soap or a compatible toy cleaner and dry fully, taking care around the USB-C port; Dame advises against alcohol-, petroleum- or acetone-based cleaners, and against boiling, baking or dishwashing the device. Use a water-based lubricant as the default with silicone, and store Dip in the included bag away from other silicone toys.

## Battery and Warranty

Dip charges over USB-C, so a standard phone-style cable will work, with Dame specifying a wall adapter rated at or below 5V. The documented run time is 1.5 hours at the highest setting from a 2-hour charge - longer at maximum settings than Pom's 1-hour runtime from the same 2-hour charge, though the two aren't directly comparable without a published battery capacity for Dip. The standout figure is the warranty: Dame documents 3 years, longer than the two-year terms common elsewhere in the category.

## In the Catalogue

### Dip vs. the Dame Arc

Both Dip and the [Arc](/products/dame-arc/) are insertable, medical-grade silicone vibrators with USB-C charging, a 3-year warranty and a matching 1.5-hour runtime. Arc is built around a curved shaft for G-spot and clitoral stimulation, with 5 pattern modes documented on its own page; Dip is the more general-purpose, lower-priced option, a straighter shape for shallow internal penetration and external play, with only a single steady pattern and a lower US list price (44.00 USD versus Arc's 119.00 USD). Buyers who want a curve aimed at the G-spot should choose Arc; buyers who want an affordable, general-purpose first toy should choose Dip.

### Dip vs. the other entry-level vibrators

Against other lower-priced, beginner-oriented vibrators in the catalogue, the [LELO Gigi 2](/products/lelo-gigi-2/) (109.00 EUR) documents a flattened G-spot tip, a 4-hour runtime and a published 79 g weight, while the [We-Vibe Tango X](/products/we-vibe-tango-x/) (89.00 EUR) is a compact external bullet with IPX7 waterproofing and 8 levels across 7 patterns. Dip undercuts both on price at 44.00 USD, but is the least feature-dense of the three, with a single steady pattern and no published weight or noise figure. Dip's case is affordability and Dame's 3-year warranty, not a feature count.

### Dip among the Dame range

Within Dame, Dip is the simplest, most affordable internal-and-external vibrator; [Pom](/products/dame-pom/) is the flexible, palm-held external device; [Arc](/products/dame-arc/) is the curved, insertable G-spot vibrator; [Com](/products/dame-com/) is the flexible-head wand; and [Aer](/products/dame-aer/) is the contactless air-pulse stimulator, with [Eva II](/products/dame-eva-ii/) the brand's hands-free wearable. Choosing between them is a question of format and budget, since all five share Dame's medical-grade silicone and multi-year warranty. The full line-up is on the [Dame reviews hub](/dame-reviews/).

## Value Position

At 44.00 USD, Dip is the most affordable Dame product reviewed here, and the price is in dollars, not euros, so European buyers should budget for currency conversion and check for import duties or a local stockist before ordering directly from Dame. The reason to choose it is specific: a documented, medical-grade silicone vibrator for internal and external use, with 5 intensity levels, a simple one-button control, USB-C charging and a 3-year warranty, at a lower price than any of its Dame siblings.
