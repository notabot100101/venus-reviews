First-Time Buyer Guide #

Buying a first pleasure product is mostly about avoiding two mistakes: unsafe materials, and paying premium prices for features you cannot evaluate yet. This page is the short path. The full decision framework lives in the how to choose guide.

Decide These Three Things First #

  1. Budget. Entry products run 50–80 EUR; mid-range 80–150 EUR brings better motors and body-safe silicone as standard. As a first buyer you rarely benefit from going above that.
  2. Noise tolerance. Thin walls or shared housing make this the deciding factor. Under 40 dB is barely audible a few feet away; our noise and discretion guide maps the ranges.
  3. Material, always. Medical-grade silicone, ABS plastic, glass or stainless steel. Nothing porous, nothing undisclosed. The body-safe materials guide explains how to verify a listing’s claims.

Before First Use #

Clean it, every time, starting before the first use: warm water and mild unscented soap, dried completely, stored clean and separate. The cleaning and maintenance guide covers cadence and which cleaners damage which materials. With silicone products, water-based lubricant is the conservative default.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should a first product cost?

Entry-level products run about 50 to 80 EUR and mid-range 80 to 150 EUR, where better motors and body-safe silicone become standard. Spending more than 150 EUR on a first product means paying for refinements you cannot evaluate yet.

What is the single most important check?

Material. Choose medical-grade silicone, ABS plastic, glass or stainless steel, and avoid porous materials like jelly or rubber and any product that does not disclose what it is made of.

Which type is easiest to start with?

External stimulators. They are less intimidating, usually compact, and easy to incorporate into partner play, which is why our starter picks are all external designs.
