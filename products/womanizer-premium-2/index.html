Womanizer Premium 2 is the reference product for pressure-wave clitoral stimulation, and it earns that position through two documented features rather than raw power. Smart Silence activates the device only when it is in contact with skin, and Autopilot 2.0 varies intensity automatically instead of holding a fixed level. Both address the practical complaints people actually have about air-pulse toys — that they are loud in the hand and monotonous at a constant setting — and neither is available on the budget alternatives.

Venus has not physically tested this product. This review is based on Womanizer’s published Premium 2 specifications, the documented warranty terms, the current Venus catalogue, and patterns in published user feedback. Where a detail is not documented clearly by the manufacturer, we say so instead of filling in the blank.

Quick Verdict #

Premium 2 is the strongest external-stimulation pick in the Venus catalogue for buyers who can meet the price. The documented 240-minute runtime against a 120-minute charge is an unusually good ratio in this category, the five-year warranty is well above the two-year norm, and Smart Silence is a genuine discretion advantage rather than a marketing line. The caveat is straightforward: it is expensive, and it does one thing. If pressure-wave stimulation does not suit your anatomy, none of the refinement helps — and the cheaper Satisfyer Pro 2 will answer that question for a fraction of the outlay.

Documented Specifications #

Per Womanizer’s published Premium 2 specifications, the device measures 155 x 50 x 35 mm and weighs 141.4 g. The material is documented as body-safe silicone made without phthalates, BPA or latex — a more explicit exclusion list than most manufacturers in this category publish, and one of the clearer material statements in the Venus catalogue.

Womanizer documents 14 intensity levels, which is a longer ladder than most air-pulse devices offer and matters because pressure-wave stimulation can go from imperceptible to overwhelming across a small range. Autopilot 2.0 alternates between intensity levels automatically. Smart Silence is documented as activating the device only on skin contact and returning it to standby when lifted away, which both quiets it and conserves battery.

Battery figures are documented as a 240-minute run time against a 120-minute charge time. Womanizer does not publish a milliamp-hour capacity in the available specifications, so the runtime figure cannot be independently sanity-checked against cell size; treat it as a manufacturer claim. The device is documented as IPX7 waterproof.

The box is listed as containing the Premium 2, a USB charging cable with no AC adaptor included, two stimulation heads in different sizes, a quick start guide, safety instructions, and a cotton storage pouch. Warranty coverage is documented as five years. The absence of a mains adaptor is a small but real omission at this price point, and the two head sizes matter more than they sound — head fit is the main variable in whether an air-pulse device works for a given person.

Materials and Body-Safety Notes #

The explicit phthalate-free, BPA-free and latex-free statement is the strongest part of Premium 2’s material documentation. Latex exclusion in particular is worth noting for anyone with a latex sensitivity, since it is rarely stated outright on toys of this type.

Body-safe silicone over the contact surfaces means non-porous where it counts and compatible with water-based lubricant. Lubricant is more relevant here than on a vibrator: a pressure-wave head needs a good seal against the skin, and a small amount of water-based lubricant around the rim is the usual way to achieve it. Avoid silicone-based lubricant unless Womanizer’s instructions specifically permit it.

IPX7 rating means the device can be rinsed rather than only wiped, which matters because the stimulation head is a concave cup that sits directly against the body. Both included heads should be removed and cleaned separately, inside and out, and dried fully before being refitted. Storing a damp head sealed onto the body is the one hygiene mistake this design invites. Keep the charging contacts dry, and inspect the silicone heads periodically — a split or perished rim will break the seal the device depends on.

Published Feedback Synthesis #

Published feedback on Premium 2 is strongly positive on effectiveness, and the consistent theme is that it is the air-pulse device people recommend once they already know they like air-pulse. Smart Silence draws specific and repeated praise, and is frequently described as the feature that makes the device usable in shared living situations — the noise only exists while it is against the body.

Autopilot divides opinion more than any other feature. Some published reports describe it as the best part of the product, removing the need to manage intensity manually; others find the randomised variation frustrating precisely because it takes control away at the wrong moment. It can be switched off, so it is a preference rather than a defect, but buyers should not treat it as a headline reason to buy.

The consistent criticism is price. Premium 2 costs substantially more than devices delivering broadly similar core stimulation, and published reviews that rate it lower almost always cite value rather than performance. The second recurring theme is the same fit dependency that affects every product in this category: the cup must seal against the anatomy for the pressure wave to work, and the two included head sizes help but do not guarantee it. Reports from people for whom neither head seals well describe the device as weak, which is a fit outcome rather than a power one.

Catalogue Comparisons #

Within the Venus catalogue, Premium 2 is the premium external-stimulation option and Satisfyer Pro 2 Generation 3 is its direct budget counterpart. The comparison is clean: Premium 2 documents 14 intensity levels, Smart Silence, Autopilot, 240 minutes of runtime and a 120-minute charge; the Pro 2 documents 11 pressure-wave programs, a roughly three-hour charge and about sixty minutes of runtime, but answers with a 15-year warranty against Premium 2’s five and a far lower price. Premium 2 is the better device; the Pro 2 is the better way to discover whether you want one.

Against LELO Sona 2, the difference is the stimulation mechanism — Sona 2 uses sonic waves, which published feedback often describes as broader and less concentrated than Womanizer’s pressure wave. That is a preference, not a ranking. Against LELO Enigma, Enigma offers air-pulse plus an insertable arm, so it is the choice for someone who wants both sensations from one device rather than the best external-only execution.

Premium 2 has no meaningful overlap with the catalogue’s insertable products. Mona 2 and GIGI 2 are G-spot vibrators, and We-Vibe Chorus and Sync are couples-oriented wearables. Anyone comparing Premium 2 against those is really choosing between stimulation categories, not between products.

Who Should Buy It #

Choose Premium 2 if you already know pressure-wave stimulation works for you, want the quietest execution of it available in this catalogue, value a documented five-year warranty and an explicit materials exclusion list, or need discretion in a shared home. The 240-minute runtime also makes it the better choice if charging frequency annoys you.

Skip it if you have not yet established that air-pulse suits you — start with the Satisfyer Pro 2 and upgrade if it does. Skip it too if you want internal stimulation, app control, or partner-facing features, none of which Premium 2 offers. Also factor in that no mains adaptor is included, so a USB power source is assumed.

Ownership Notes #

Try both stimulation heads before concluding anything about the device. Head fit is the single biggest determinant of whether a pressure-wave toy works, and the smaller and larger cups produce meaningfully different results for different anatomies.

Clean both heads separately after use and let them dry fully before refitting. Keep the USB cable with the device given that no adaptor is supplied. Save proof of purchase and register the product: five years is long enough that the retailer record will be hard to reconstruct if you need to claim, and the warranty is one of the clearer reasons to buy this rather than a cheaper alternative.
