LELO GIGI 2 is a premium G-spot vibrator built around one distinguishing feature: a flattened, angled tip rather than the rounded bulb most insertable vibrators use. That flat face is the whole design argument. It spreads contact over a broader area of the front wall instead of concentrating it at a single point, which some people find more effective and others find less precise. GIGI 2 is also noticeably smaller and lighter than LELO’s other G-spot toys, which makes it the more practical option for anyone who wants a premium insertable device that packs down.

This review is based on LELO’s published GIGI 2 specifications, LELO’s warranty and materials documentation, the current product catalogue, and patterns in published user feedback.

Quick Verdict #

GIGI 2 is a good shortlist pick for shoppers who want a compact, waterproof, premium-feeling G-spot vibrator with a documented material stack and LELO’s warranty structure behind it. The flattened tip is a genuine point of difference rather than marketing, and the 79 g weight makes it one of the easier premium insertables to travel with. It is harder to recommend to someone who wants power above all, or who wants app control, dual stimulation or external coverage — GIGI 2 does one job, and its 8 settings are a modest feature set for the price.

Documented Specifications #

Per LELO’s published GIGI 2 specifications, the device measures 163 x 35 x 42 mm, or 6.5 x 1.3 x 1.2 in. LELO lists the insertable length as 95 mm, or 3.7 in, and the diameter as 37.8 mm, or 1.5 in. The documented weight is 79 g, or 2.8 oz — light enough that the difference against a heavier premium toy is noticeable in the hand.

Materials are documented as body-safe silicone with ABS plastic. LELO lists the battery as a Li-ion 520 mAh 3.7 V cell, with a charge time of 2 hours at 5.0 V 500 mA, user time of up to 4 hours, and standby time of up to 90 days. The interface offers 8 vibration settings, and the product is documented as 100% waterproof. The box is listed as containing the GIGI 2, a USB charging cord, a satin storage pouch, a warranty registration card, and an instruction manual.

Two gaps are worth naming. LELO does not publish a decibel figure for GIGI 2 in the available specification table, unlike Mona 2, where a 50 dB maximum is documented. Anyone buying primarily for quiet operation is therefore buying without a manufacturer number to hold LELO to. LELO also does not document a travel lock in that table, which matters for a toy whose size invites packing.

Warranty follows LELO’s standard structure: a two-year replacement warranty from the purchase date for defects, followed by a ten-year quality guarantee period. Keep the receipt, because the claim process depends on it. Retailer pricing should be checked at purchase time.

Materials and Body-Safety Notes #

The silicone-over-ABS construction is the same stack LELO documents across its premium range, and it is the right one for an insertable product. The body-contact surface should be non-porous, straightforward to clean, and compatible with water-based lubricant. Silicone lubricants are the usual exception — stay with water-based unless LELO’s instructions for this specific product say otherwise.

The 100% waterproof rating does real work on an insertable toy. It means the device can be washed properly rather than wiped, and that water around the button area is not a failure risk. Dry the charging contacts before storing or charging, and inspect the silicone periodically for cuts or tackiness; damage to the surface is what turns a non-porous material into a porous one.

GIGI 2’s insertable length of 95 mm is modest, and the flattened tip means the widest dimension is not circular. Buyers who are used to judging fit by diameter alone should read the 37.8 mm figure alongside the 35 x 42 mm cross-section rather than assuming a round profile. LELO does not document phthalate content explicitly in the available specification table, though the body-safe silicone claim is the material assurance the company leads with.

Published Feedback Synthesis #

Published feedback on GIGI 2 divides almost entirely along the axis of the flattened tip. Users who like it describe the broader contact as more satisfying and less jabbing than a pointed G-spot toy, and often say it is the design that finally made G-spot stimulation work for them. Users who do not like it describe it as vague or insufficiently targeted, and prefer a more pronounced curve of the sort Mona 2 offers.

The second recurring theme is power. GIGI 2’s motor is generally described as pleasant and reasonably rumbly but not among the strongest available, and published reports from people who prefer intense vibration frequently suggest it is underpowered for the money. Its 8 settings also read as a short list next to devices offering more patterns or app-driven customisation, and that comparison comes up often in reviews that criticise the price.

Positive feedback clusters on build quality, the light weight, the waterproof rating, and the storage pouch — the ownership experience rather than raw performance. The absence of a published noise figure is worth restating here: user reports describe GIGI 2 as fairly quiet, but “fairly quiet” from reviewers is not a manufacturer specification, and buyers with a hard discretion requirement should treat it as unverified.

Catalogue Comparisons #

GIGI 2 is the compact premium insertable. The closest comparison is LELO Mona 2, and the two are genuinely different products despite the shared brand and category. Mona 2 is larger at 220 mm with a 110 mm insertable length, heavier at 128 g, offers 6 modes with a documented 50 dB maximum, and uses a pronounced rounded curve. GIGI 2 is smaller, lighter, offers 8 settings, and uses the flat tip. Choose Mona 2 for firm targeted pressure and a documented noise figure; choose GIGI 2 for broader contact and portability.

Against LELO Enigma, GIGI 2 is the narrower product — Enigma combines air-pulse external stimulation with an insertable arm, which is a fuller feature set for someone who wants both sensations. Against LELO Sona 2, there is no real overlap: Sona 2 is external only. Against Dame Eva II, GIGI 2 is the more premium build but the less versatile device, since Eva II’s positioning is hands-free and partner-friendly. Buyers looking primarily for external or suction-style stimulation should look at Womanizer Premium 2 or Satisfyer Pro 2 instead.

Who Should Buy It #

Choose GIGI 2 if you want a premium, compact, fully waterproof G-spot vibrator, are drawn to the flattened tip’s broader contact, value LELO’s documented materials and warranty structure, and want something genuinely easy to pack. It is also a reasonable step up from a cheap insertable vibrator that felt buzzy or poorly finished.

Skip it if you want maximum power, need app control or a long pattern library, want dual or external stimulation, or need a documented noise figure before buying. Also skip it if you specifically want the pronounced curve of a classic G-spot wand — that is Mona 2’s design, not this one, and the flat tip is not a substitute for it.

Ownership Notes #

Use the satin pouch. A light silicone toy stored loose in a drawer collects lint quickly, and GIGI 2’s flat face is a larger surface for it to cling to. Charge before long storage even though LELO documents up to 90 days of standby, since lithium-ion cells age better when not left flat.

Keep proof of purchase and register the product while the retailer record is still easy to find — the two-year replacement window and the ten-year guarantee period both depend on it. If you plan to travel with it, confirm the lock behaviour in the printed manual before you pack it, because the specification table does not document a travel lock.
