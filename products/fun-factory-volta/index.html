Quick Verdict #

Volta suits someone who wants precise, variable external contact rather than a fixed point of stimulation or broad wand coverage: two silicone tips can be used together, apart, or against different spots, and 6 speeds plus 6 patterns give real room to vary the feel. Fun Factory documents a fully measured 190 g, 63 x 47 x 191 mm body in body-friendly, hygienic silicone, IPX7 waterproofing and USB-C charging, at 51.95 EUR with all three colourways - Aquamarine, Black and Pink - in stock when checked. Against that, no runtime, charge time, warranty term or noise figure is published, and the twin-tip shape is less immediately familiar than a bullet or a conventional lay-on vibrator.

Format and Who It Is For #

Volta is a lay-on external vibrator distinguished by its two flexible, fluttering silicone tips rather than a single contact surface. Fun Factory’s feature list frames it as a clitoris vibrator first, with the tips placeable on either side of the clitoris, above it, spread along the labia, or with one tip near the vaginal entrance - the page explicitly invites experimentation with placement. A ribbed LOOP handle, shared with the Big Boss and Miss Bi, gives a secure grip for holding a steady angle.

It suits buyers who enjoy varying pressure, angle and contact style and are comfortable with a shape that takes a little longer to learn than a standard external vibrator. It is less suitable for anyone who wants broad-surface wand coverage, simple pinpoint bullet stimulation, or a device with no learning curve at all, and for anyone who specifically needs a documented runtime or warranty before buying.

  • Vibration: 6 speeds and 6 patterns (“6 Vibrationsmuster treffen auf 6 Geschwindigkeiten”)

Not documented: runtime, charge time, noise level in decibels, warranty term, app or remote control.

Materials, Waterproofing and Cleaning #

The tips and body are body-friendly, hygienic silicone over a handle that houses the controls. Silicone is non-porous and suitable for external use when cleaned correctly; water-based lubricant is the conservative choice, since silicone-based lubricant can degrade a silicone toy’s surface. The IPX7 rating means Volta can be rinsed under running water with mild soap or a toy cleaner and used in the bath or shower, and unlike the VIM page, no FAQ language on this page contradicts that waterproofing claim.

The twin-tip design and ribbed handle create more surface detail than a smooth external vibrator, which means more places for lubricant residue to collect. Pay particular attention to the gap between the tips and the texture of the handle grip, and dry both fully before storage.

Battery and Warranty #

Volta runs on a built-in rechargeable battery over USB-C, so any standard cable works. That is where the documentation stops: no runtime or charge time is published for this model, and no warranty term appears on the page, a gap shared by the VIM and Manta pages. Buyers who want a specific battery life or a warranty window should check Fun Factory’s manual or ask the retailer first.

In the Catalogue #

Volta vs. the Fun Factory externals #

Within Fun Factory’s own range, Volta sits between the smaller Laya III (85 g, 32.95 EUR, 7 speeds, 3 patterns) and the larger Manta (287 g, 48.95 EUR, 6 speeds, 6 patterns, currently out of stock at the manufacturer): Volta is the more targeted, precision-focused shape, where Manta is built for broader wraparound contact and Laya III for simple palm-held use. The VIM wand (437 g, 66.95 EUR) is a different format entirely, built for broad-surface massage rather than pinpoint control.

Volta vs. other precision external vibrators #

On the best mini & bullet vibrators page, Volta’s closest comparisons are toys built around a distinctive contact shape rather than a simple bullet tip. Volta’s documentation advantage is its fully measured dimensions and weight and its consistent IPX7 claim; its gap, shared with most of the Fun Factory catalogue reviewed here, is the missing runtime, charge time and warranty figures that some competitors publish.

Volta among the Fun Factory range #

Across the Fun Factory models Venus has reviewed, Volta is the only one with a genuinely split-tip contact surface, distinct from the Big Boss G-spot vibrator, the wand-format VIM, and the flexible-wing Manta. The full line-up is on the Fun Factory reviews hub.

Value Position #

At 51.95 EUR direct from the manufacturer, with no discount and all three colourways in stock when checked, Volta sits in the mid-range of Fun Factory’s external vibrators and qualifies for the budget picks page alongside the brand’s other sub-60 EUR devices. The price buys a fully measured, IPX7, USB-C-rechargeable vibrator with a documented 6-speed, 6-pattern motor and a genuinely distinctive twin-tip shape. It does not buy a documented runtime, charge time, warranty or decibel noise figure. It rates 4.0, reflecting solid size, material and motor documentation offset by the missing battery and warranty figures and a shape that needs a short learning curve. Buyers weighing format first should start with the catalogue overview.
