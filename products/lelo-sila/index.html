LELO Sila is the gentle entry in LELO’s sonic range: the same non-contact sonic-wave category as Sona 2, tuned for a slower, softer build rather than maximum intensity. It answers a request the existing pages do not: an air-pulse-family product specifically positioned for users who find the standard intensity curves too aggressive.

Venus has not physically tested this product. This draft is based on LELO’s own product page (lelo.com/sila, checked 6 August 2026, and re-verified 20 August 2026) and the product’s relationship to the already-reviewed Sona 2. Where a detail is not documented in that capture, this page says so.

Quick Verdict #

Sila is the considered recommendation for sensitivity-first shoppers: LELO’s own positioning is “the gentle clitoral vibrator”, with a wide mouth that spreads its sonic waves over a larger area than Sona 2’s focused opening. At a verified 169 EUR it costs more than Sona 2 (109 EUR) — the premium buys the gentler tuning and larger contact area, not more power. Buyers chasing intensity should buy Sona 2 and save 60 EUR.

Sonic-Wave Format in Context #

Sonic-wave and pressure-wave toys form a distinct subcategory of external stimulation. Unlike vibrators that produce physical oscillation through a motor, sonic-wave devices use a diaphragm that pulses air through a nozzle or opening. The sensation is typically described as pulsating, tapping, or fluttering rather than buzzing — a difference in kind, not just degree, from conventional vibration.

LELO’s Sona 2 established the company’s approach to this format: a focused 23 mm mouth, 12 intensity levels, and a maximum noise ceiling of 60 dB. Sila takes the same core technology and re-tunes it: the mouth is significantly wider, spreading the sonic pulses over a larger surface area, and the intensity curve is softer. LELO’s own product-page language calls it “a new way to enjoy a teasing climax” and positions it specifically as the gentler option within the same family.

This matters for catalogue coverage. The existing Venus reviews cover Sona 2 (focused/intense), Womanizer Premium 2 (pressure-wave with Smart Silence/Autopilot), and Satisfyer Pro 2 (budget pressure-wave). Sila fills a gap that none of these address: a premium sonic device explicitly designed for users who find the format too intense.

Design and Build #

Sila uses body-safe silicone and ABS plastic — the same material combination as Sona 2 and the standard for premium external stimulators at this price point. LELO describes the silicone as “extra-soft”, which is a plausible material-grade difference from the standard silicone used in Sona 2 and would contribute to the gentler overall character of the device. The silicone feels warm to the touch per LELO’s own description.

The device measures 80 x 75 x 35 mm (3.1 x 2.9 x 1.4 inches) — noticeably broader and shorter than Sona 2’s 112 x 55 x 51 mm dimensions, consistent with the wider-mouth design. Weight is 105 g (0.23 lb), making it one of the lighter products in the Venus catalogue and easily portable.

The 3-button interface is straightforward: power, intensity up, intensity down. Sila offers 8 sonic-wave intensity levels, compared to Sona 2’s 12, which is consistent with the design brief — fewer levels to navigate when the range is deliberately gentler. An automatic switch-off after 20 minutes of inactivity is standard for this category and helps preserve battery life.

Battery and Charging #

Sila uses a Li-ion 520 mAh 3.7 V battery, the same capacity as Sona 2. Charging takes 2 hours at 5.0 V / 500 mA and uses a standard USB charging cord. User time is documented at up to 2 hours per charge, with standby up to 90 days.

These figures are competitive for the category. A 2-hour runtime on a full charge supports multiple sessions before recharging, and the 2-hour charge time is reasonable for overnight top-ups. The USB charging cord that ships with Sila is a common standard, though the type of connector (USB-A to proprietary or USB-A to pin charger) is not documented from the available capture.

Waterproofing and Cleaning #

LELO describes Sila as 100% waterproof. This supports cleaning under running water with soap or toy cleaner, which is the recommended practice for external sonic-wave devices. The wide mouth opening and silicone contact surface are the areas that need the most attention during cleaning: sonic-wave openings can trap lubricant and fluids between the diaphragm and the outer rim, so thorough rinsing and complete drying before storage are important.

Storage in the included satin pouch (per LELO’s standard packaging for this price tier) helps protect the silicone surface from lint and dust. Water-based lubricant is recommended as with all silicone-bodied toys; silicone lubricant can interact with the silicone surface, and oil-based products may be difficult to fully remove from the device’s opening.

Noise Considerations #

LELO documents a maximum noise level of 60 dB for Sona 2, its comparable sonic model. A comparable figure for Sila is not documented in the available capture. Given that Sila uses the same motor and battery platform at a lower intensity profile, the noise level is likely similar or slightly lower than Sona 2’s, but this is an inference rather than a documented specification. Buyers who need absolute silence should consider the noise level an unconfirmed variable until independent measurements are available.

Community feedback on external sonic stimulators broadly places them in a range where the sound is audible in a quiet room but not through a closed door at conversational distance. This is consistent with the 60 dB figure for Sona 2 — comparable to a quiet conversation or running water.

In the Catalogue #

Sila vs. Sona 2 #

This is the most direct comparison in the catalogue. Both are LELO sonic-wave devices using the same battery platform and core technology, differentiated primarily by mouth width and intensity tuning:

At a 60 EUR premium, the question is straightforward: does a gentler tuning justify the price gap? For users who have tried focused sonic stimulation and found it too sharp or intense, the answer is clearly yes — no other product in the catalogue offers the same design brief. For anyone unsure, Sona 2 at 109 EUR is the safer first purchase because its intensity curve can be dialled down, while Sila’s upper range is inherently lower.

Sila vs. Satisfyer Pro 2 #

Satisfyer Pro 2 (Generation 3) occupies the budget end of the pressure-wave spectrum at 69.95 EUR. The price gap with Sila is nearly 100 EUR. Satisfyer’s Liquid Air technology delivers pressure-wave stimulation at a fraction of the cost, with IPX7 waterproofing and a broader range of included accessories.

The trade-off is in materials, build tolerance, brand warranty depth, and the overall refinement of the experience. LELO positions Sila explicitly as a premium product with documented materials (ISO 3533 language) and a multi-year warranty program. Satisfyer delivers comparable core functionality at less than half the price. The buyer who values documented quality assurance and is willing to pay for it chooses Sila; the pragmatic buyer who wants pressure-wave stimulation at the best available price chooses Satisfyer.

Sila vs. Womanizer Premium 2 #

Womanizer Premium 2 at 179 EUR is Sila’s closest price competitor at the premium end. Womanizer offers Smart Silence (contact-based activation) and Autopilot 2.0 (automatic intensity cycling) — features that Sila does not match. Womanizer also carries a 5-year warranty and IPX7 waterproofing.

Sila’s advantage is the specific gentleness tuning. Womanizer Premium 2 is a capable, feature-rich device, but its design brief is feature density rather than gentleness. Buyers specifically seeking the gentlest possible sonic experience — the “teasing” pacing that LELO markets — will find Sila better aligned with that goal.

Value Position #

At 169 EUR, Sila sits in the upper-middle of the Venus catalogue price range. It is more expensive than Sona 2 (109 EUR) and Satisfyer Pro 2 (69.95 EUR), comparable to the We-Vibe Sync (179 EUR promo), and cheaper than LELO’s own Hugo (189 EUR) and Womanizer Premium 2 (179 EUR).

The value question depends entirely on the buyer’s sensitivity profile. For someone who knows they want gentle external sonic stimulation, Sila is the only product in the catalogue that specifically addresses that need — it cannot be replaced by a cheaper alternative without changing the experience. For a buyer who is merely curious about sonic stimulation, Sona 2 or Satisfyer Pro 2 offer better value because the core format is the same even if the tuning differs.

Who It Is For #

  • Sensitivity-first shoppers for whom “more power” is a bug, not a feature
  • Buyers who want documented premium materials (LELO’s ISO 3533 alignment)

Who Should Look Elsewhere #
