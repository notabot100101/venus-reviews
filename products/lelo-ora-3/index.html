LELO ORA 3 is the catalogue’s one rotating external stimulator. LELO calls it an intelligent oral sex simulator: a nub rotates under a soft surface, driven by what LELO names PreMotion technology, with vibration added on top. It is the third generation; LELO’s page claims it is 25% faster than the previous one without saying what was measured, so that figure is treated as marketing, not a specification.

Venus has not physically tested this product. This review is based on LELO’s official ORA 3 product page and its published specification block, checked on 13 October 2026. Where LELO does not document something clearly, this page says so rather than filling the gap.

Quick Verdict #

ORA 3 is the pick for buyers who want movement against the skin instead of vibration alone or air pulses. The specification block is complete: silicone and ABS, 83.5 x 88 x 43.5 mm, 130 g, an exact 50 dB maximum noise level, 100% waterproofing, twelve settings and a two-year warranty, in stock at 179 EUR with no discount shown when checked. The caveats are a documented user time of only up to one hour, a contact point that depends on placement by design, and a price level with the Womanizer Premium 2 and 70 EUR above the Sona 2 for a narrower use case.

Format and Who It Is For #

ORA 3 is an external-only stimulator, with the rotating nub and its surrounding surface placed on or around the clitoris. LELO’s how-to copy starts with its Personal Moisturizer, moves ORA 3 around the clitoris “to build anticipation”, then directly onto it, and suggests combining it with a second vibrator. There is no insertable element and no second arm.

That makes it a device for someone who finds fixed vibration too static, or who owns a bullet or air-pulse device and wants a different sensation rather than a stronger one. LELO describes the nub as a “firm, controlled tongue-like feeling”. The format’s weakness is structural: a nub that moves needs to sit where the user wants it, so this is a position-sensitive device and not a first toy. One line of LELO’s how-to copy mentions “sonic sensations”, but the highlights and specification block describe rotation and vibration only, so no sonic technology is recorded here.

Documented Specifications #

LELO’s specification block lists body-safe silicone and ABS plastic with a matte and glossy finish, 83.5 x 88 x 43.5 mm and 130 g. Power is a lithium-ion 3.7 V 420 mAh cell, charged in two hours at 5.0 V and 400 mA, for up to one hour of user time and up to 90 days of standby. The motor frequency is documented at 100 Hz, the device switches off automatically after 20 minutes, and the maximum noise level is stated as an exact 50 dB rather than a ceiling.

The feature tiles add twelve settings, “from a teasing murmur to a satisfying pulse”, ISO 3533 safety assurance and an award-winning design. LELO does not split the twelve into intensity levels and patterns, so this page records twelve settings and nothing more precise. Unusually for a LELO page there is no interface line, so the button count is not documented; nor are app control, the number of motors, the nub’s dimensions or an IP rating beyond “100% waterproof”.

Materials, Waterproofing and Cleaning #

LELO marks ORA 3 as 100% waterproof and suitable for the bath and shower, which matters more here than on a simple vibrator: the moving nub and the contours around it collect lubricant, so rinse under running water with mild soap, pay attention to the nub, and dry fully before it goes into the supplied satin pouch. The contact surface is body-safe silicone, non-porous and straightforward to keep clean, with ABS plastic listed alongside it. LELO states ISO 3533 certification, the international safety standard for sex toys. No IP rating is published, only the waterproof statement. Water-based lubricant is the conservative default on silicone unless LELO’s instructions say otherwise.

Battery and Warranty #

The two-hour charge gives a documented one hour of use, with up to 90 days of standby and an automatic switch-off after 20 minutes. One hour is half the figure LELO publishes for most of its range, so ORA 3 is a device to charge regularly rather than leave in a drawer. LELO documents a two-year warranty from the purchase date, with a free replacement if a problem occurs in that time, followed by a ten-year quality guarantee once it expires. Keep the receipt; LELO’s warranty text asks for it.

In the Catalogue #

ORA 3 vs. the LELO Sona 2 #

The LELO Sona 2 is the catalogue’s top sonic pick at 109 EUR: sonic waves rather than contact, the same twelve settings, 125 g, 100% waterproofing and a two-year warranty, with a documented two-hour runtime and a noise ceiling under 60 dB. ORA 3 answers with physical movement and an exact 50 dB figure, for 70 EUR more and half the runtime. Buyers who want sonic stimulation without contact should keep the Sona 2 at the top of the list; buyers who want a moving nub are ORA 3’s audience.

ORA 3 vs. the LELO Sila #

The LELO Sila at 169 EUR is LELO’s gentler, wide-mouthed sonic stimulator with eight settings, a 105 g body, a two-hour runtime and 100% waterproofing; the catalogue’s Sila review records no noise figure or warranty term. ORA 3 is 10 EUR more, documents more, and is the more specialised: the Sila’s wide mouth is easier to position, ORA 3’s nub asks for precision and offers a sensation the Sila cannot imitate.

ORA 3 vs. the air-pulse field #

The Womanizer Premium 2 costs the same 179 EUR and documents a four-hour runtime, 14 intensity levels, an IPX7 rating and 141.4 g, but no noise figure; it is the safer pick for buyers who know air-pulse stimulation works for them, ORA 3 the alternative for those who dislike a cup over the clitoris. The We-Vibe Tango X bullet at 89 EUR documents eight levels, seven patterns, IPX7 and a two-year warranty for half the price. External stimulators across brands are ranked on the best air-pulse stimulators page, the LELO line-up is on the LELO reviews hub, and the catalogue-wide ranking, where ORA 3 holds the rotating-format slot, is in best vibrators 2026.

Value Position #

At a 179 EUR list price, in stock with no discount shown when checked, ORA 3 sits in the catalogue’s premium external band alongside the Womanizer Premium 2 and 10 EUR above the Sila. It is not a value pick: the Sona 2 documents the same settings count, twice the runtime and the same waterproofing for 109 EUR. What the extra money buys is specificity, a rotating contact point no other product in the catalogue offers, with an exact noise figure and a complete materials and warranty record behind it. Buyers who want a conventional first external toy should start with the Sona 2 or a bullet; buyers who already own one and want a different kind of stimulation have a clear reason to shortlist ORA 3, provided they accept the one-hour runtime.
