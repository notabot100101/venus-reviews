Best for Technology

App control, remotes and connected features that earn their place. Which technology in intimate products is genuinely useful and which is a battery drain.

Best for Technology #

Connectivity in intimate products splits cleanly into features that solve a real problem and features that exist to fill a specification sheet. Remote control solves a genuine problem: it lets someone else operate the device, or lets you operate it without contorting. App control solves a narrower one — long-distance use, and pattern customisation for people who actually want it.

The picks below are the catalogue’s genuinely connected devices. Venus has not physically tested them; each links to a full review built from manufacturer documentation, including what the manufacturer declines to document.

What Connected Features Actually Do #

Remote control is the most reliably useful. It works without a phone, without an account, and without a firmware update. For partnered use it is the whole point.

App control adds long-distance operation and custom patterns. It also adds an account, a Bluetooth pairing step and a dependency on the manufacturer continuing to maintain the app — a real consideration for a product you expect to own for years.

Motion-sensitive remotes map physical gestures to intensity instead of button presses.

Contact and motion sensing lets a device respond to what is happening rather than holding a fixed setting.

The honest caveat that applies to all of it: connected features consume battery, add failure modes, and are worth having only if you will use them. A well-made device with buttons beats a connected device you stop pairing after a fortnight.

Our Technology Picks #

1. We-Vibe Chorus — Most Complete Connected System #

Chorus is the most app-and-remote-centred device in the catalogue. It is built around partnered positioning with adjustable fit, a Squeeze Remote, Touch-sense modes and We-Vibe app connectivity — a genuinely layered control system rather than a single connected feature.

The Squeeze Remote is the detail worth highlighting: squeezing it harder increases intensity, which maps a physical gesture to a control rather than requiring you to find a button in the dark.

The caveat: capability comes with dependency. Chorus needs setup, correct fit, charging discipline, and a genuine appetite for the app-and-remote layer. If you would not use it, this is the wrong device.

2. We-Vibe Sync — Simpler Connected Wearable #

Sync is the more restrained sibling. We-Vibe’s documentation presents it plainly: adjustable fit, remote control, IPX7 waterproof construction, USB charging, app control, and a compact C-shaped body for shared stimulation.

If Chorus sounds like more system than you want, Sync delivers the core connected wearable proposition — remote and app control on an adjustable body — without the additional control layers.

The caveat: it is still a wearable whose value depends almost entirely on fit, and adjustability is not the same as fitting everyone.

3. Lelo Hugo — Best Motion-Controlled Remote #

Hugo is a rechargeable, waterproof, remote-controlled prostate massager with documented dimensions, silicone and ABS materials, and LELO’s motion-sensitive remote concept — tilting the remote changes intensity rather than pressing through levels.

It is the clearest example in the catalogue of a remote designed around gesture rather than buttons, and LELO documents the materials and warranty language to its usual standard.

The caveat: this is a prostate-specific device, not a general-purpose connected toy. It belongs on this list for the remote, not as an alternative to the wearables above.

Connected Does Not Mean Better #

If app control is not something you will use, the catalogue’s best-engineered devices are not the connected ones. Womanizer Premium 2 has no app at all and is the most refined external stimulator here. Lelo Mona 2 has six modes, four buttons and a published noise figure.

Buy connectivity because you want the specific thing it enables — partnered control, distance, or gesture input. Buying it as a proxy for quality gets you a shorter battery life and an app you will stop opening.
